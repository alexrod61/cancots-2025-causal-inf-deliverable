# Canadian Conference on Teaching Statistics (CanCOTS) 2025

## Incorporating Causal Inference in Statistics Courses

**Held by:** [Statistical Education Section](https://ssc.ca/en/about/sections-regions/statistical-education) of the [Statistical Society of Canada (SSC)](https://ssc.ca/en).

**Dates:** Wednesday and Thursday, June 11 and 12, 2025.

**Place:** HEC Montréal, Québec, Canada.

**Working group lead during the CanCOTS sessions and final deliverable curator:** [G. Alexi Rodríguez-Arelis](https://alexrod.netlify.app/) (UBC).

**Working group contributors during the CanCOTS sessions:** [Philippe Boileau](https://pboileau.ca/) (McGill University), [Irene Vrbik](https://irene.vrbik.ok.ubc.ca/about/) (UBC), and Nicole Babor.

## Overview

This repository contains the final deliverable developed from the CanCOTS 2025 working group on incorporating causal inference in statistics courses. The initial direction and outline were shaped collaboratively during the conference working-group sessions. The final public-facing deliverable was subsequently expanded, organized, and completed after the conference by G. Alexi Rodríguez-Arelis.

## Contents

The deliverable includes:
- introductory slides
- a course outline
- a roadmap for integrating causal inference into statistics courses
- lesson-plan material
- conference background and supporting context

## Use of Large Language Models 

After the workgroup sessions at CanCOTS 2025, a large language model (LLM), such as ChatGPT [@chatgpt], was used as a supporting tool during parts of the curriculum-development process. It proved moderately useful for brainstorming and organizing lecture-level learning goals in ways that remained aligned with the pedagogical direction established during the workgroup sessions. The LLM was also used to surface scholarly references that could be considered as potential teaching and learning resources beyond the textbooks identified in the [introductory slides](website/01-intro-slides.qmd).

This use of the LLM required substantial human oversight. In particular, suggested references were carefully checked against their original sources, since fabricated or inaccurate citations were a recurring limitation of LLM-generated outputs at the time. Each resource was therefore independently verified for existence, scholarly credibility, relevance to the intended learning goals, and consistency with the scope and level of the proposed course before being incorporated into the deliverable. This process reflects a broader educational leadership principle adopted throughout the project: generative tools may support resource discovery, but decisions about curriculum, pedagogy, evidence, and scholarly quality remain the responsibility of the educators designing and reviewing the learning experience.
